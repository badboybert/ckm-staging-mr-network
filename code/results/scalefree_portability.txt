=== Scale-free cross-ancestry concordance ===

Edges touching HbA1c or eGFR are excluded throughout (scale dictionary: non-comparable).
SBP needs care, and the first draft of this script got it wrong:
  * SIGN concordance and the Z-STATISTIC (b/se) are genuinely scale-free - b and se rescale
    together - so SBP edges are KEPT for those two.
  * SPEARMAN and PEARSON are computed over a POOLED set, so rescaling one subset changes the
    pooled ranking too; neither is scale-free here. SBP is therefore CONVERTED (x19.3) for
    those two, and a no-SBP variant is also reported to reproduce the shipped 0.681/0.657.

READ:
- The shipped magnitude definition (comparable edges, SBP dropped) is REPRODUCED exactly:
  0.681 over 56 edges and 0.657 over 37. Implementation and shipped number agree.
- CORRECTION to an earlier draft of this script: it computed the scale-free statistics on the
  magnitude-ELIGIBLE subset, i.e. on a set selected by a scale criterion, which is self-
  defeating. Over all 101 common edges the sign concordance is 0.614 and 0.692, which are
  EXACTLY the manuscript's shipped values in results/h3_portability.txt - so there is one
  number for this statistic, not two.
- The z-statistic correlation FALLS when the excluded edges are restored (+0.77 -> +0.60 and
  +0.80 -> +0.59), i.e. below the magnitude correlation, so the earlier claim that it was
  'the highest of the three' was an artifact of the filter. It is also largely a shared-POWER
  statistic - correlating |z| alone recovers nearly the same value - and is dominated by
  near-definitional lipid edges (LDL->TC carries z_EUR = 71.7). Report it as supportive at
  most, never as evidence of stronger portability.
- Cohort-specific SDs are not recoverable from the published summary statistics on either
  side, which is why a sign-level reading is the defensible one for the non-comparable traits.
